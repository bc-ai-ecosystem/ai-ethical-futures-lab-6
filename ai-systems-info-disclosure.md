# 3. Improving the Availability of Consistent and Understandable Information about AI Systems

## Challenge
Users and businesses need reliable information on intended uses, performance, limitations, evaluation results and (at appropriate granularity) training-data characteristics. General-purpose models differ from specialised systems; full dataset disclosure can harm innovation and security.

## Questions
* What kinds of information about AI systems would help individuals make informed choices? For which types of products is this most important?  
* What information do businesses need to adopt and use AI safely and responsibly? Do they currently have adequate access?
* How should the Government balance greater transparency (including input transparency) against protection of confidential business information?
* Who in the AI value chain is best placed to provide information about how AI systems are created, their capabilities and limitations? Are existing disclosure practices and legal frameworks sufficient? If not, where are the gaps and what actions should the Government take?

## Existing Practical Solutions
Model cards and system cards (Hugging Face Model Cards are widely adopted; commercial providers publish analogous safety reports). Datasheets for Datasets. Emerging ISO/IEC, NIST and IEEE documentation standards. Canada’s Voluntary Code already encourages publication of capabilities and limitations.

## EU AI Act
For **general-purpose AI (GPAI) models**, Article 53 requires providers to draw up and keep up-to-date technical documentation covering training and testing processes and evaluation results (minimum content in Annex XI) and to make available information and documentation to downstream providers who integrate the model (Annex XII). Providers must also publish a sufficiently detailed summary of the content used for training. A Code of Practice and Model Documentation Form facilitate compliance while protecting intellectual property.

For **high-risk AI systems**, more extensive transparency and documentation obligations apply (technical documentation, instructions for use, logging, etc.). Compliant providers maintain living documentation, supply downstream providers with the information needed to meet their own obligations, and publish high-level training summaries. This creates a cascading transparency regime without requiring full public disclosure of proprietary weights or datasets.
