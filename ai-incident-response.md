# 4. Enabling the Tracking of Serious Incidents Related to AI Systems

## Challenge
Failures, near-misses and harms will increase with adoption. Without structured visibility, lessons cannot be shared and policy cannot be evidence-based. Cross-border systems and liability concerns complicate regimes.

## Questions
* Do the public and Government currently have enough information about AI risks, incidents and emerging harms? What types of information on AI use and risk trends would be most useful?  
* How should a “serious AI incident” be defined? What information is most useful for understanding causes, consequences and mitigation? How should reporting requirements be structured to encourage proactive disclosure while addressing confidentiality and liability? Who is best placed to provide the information, who should collect it, and what should be done with it?  
* Are existing Canadian incident-reporting frameworks sufficient for serious AI incidents? If not, where are the gaps and what actions should the Government take? Are there international best practices Canada should adapt or interoperate with?

## Existing Practical Solutions
- **AI Incident Database (AIID)** (Partnership on AI / Responsible AI Collaborative): >1,200 crowdsourced real-world harms.
- **OECD AI Incidents and Hazards Monitor (AIM)**: real-time media-based tracking with a common reporting framework.
- Sectoral Canadian regimes (medical devices, transportation, consumer products, cybersecurity).
- Emerging confidential industry sharing mechanisms.

## EU AI Act
Article 73 requires providers of high-risk AI systems to report any serious incident to the market surveillance authorities of the Member States where the incident occurred. Reporting must occur immediately after establishing a causal link (or reasonable likelihood) and in any event not later than 15 days after becoming aware; shorter deadlines apply for deaths (10 days) or widespread/critical-infrastructure incidents (2 days). Follow-up investigation and corrective-action duties apply.

For GPAI models with systemic risk, Article 55 requires providers to track, document and report serious incidents (and possible corrective measures) to the AI Office and, where appropriate, national authorities, without undue delay. The Commission has published reporting templates and draft guidance. Compliant providers maintain internal incident-tracking systems, use the official templates, and report within the strict timelines while protecting legitimate confidentiality interests.
